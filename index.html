EA

Enrolled Agent

The highest credential the IRS awards. Enrolled Agents are federally licensed tax practitioners who have demonstrated expertise across every area of taxation and hold unlimited rights to represent taxpayers before the IRS.

Why it matters

A tax specialist who can stand beside you.

1

Licensed by the U.S. Treasury

EAs earn their license by passing a comprehensive IRS exam covering individual and business tax, plus representation.

2

Unlimited representation rights

An EA can speak with the IRS on your behalf for audits, collections and appeals — in any state.

3

Always current

Enrolled Agents must complete continuing education every year to keep up with changing tax law.

Good questions

Frequently asked

What's the difference between an Enrolled Agent and a CPA?+

Both are licensed professionals. CPAs are licensed by states and often focus on auditing and general accounting. Enrolled Agents are licensed by the IRS and specialize specifically in taxation and representing taxpayers before the IRS. For tax preparation and IRS issues, an EA is a specialist.

What should I bring for my tax appointment?+

Typically: photo ID, Social Security numbers for everyone on the return, W-2s and 1099s, mortgage and property-tax statements, records of deductible expenses, any IRS letters, and last year's return. We'll send a personalized checklist after your consultation.

I got a scary letter from the IRS. What should I do?+

Don't ignore it, and don't pay or respond until you understand it. Many notices have response deadlines, so reach out as soon as you can. We'll review the letter, explain what it means and handle the response.

I haven't filed in several years. Can you help?+

Yes. Getting back into compliance is very common and very fixable. We'll pull your IRS records, prepare the missing returns, and work out a plan for any balance owed.

Should my LLC elect to be taxed as an S corporation?+

It depends on your profit level, how you pay yourself and your state. For some owners it saves meaningful self-employment tax; for others the added payroll costs outweigh the benefit. We'll run the numbers for your specific situation.
